Reflection

The Threshold:

A specific moment where code clicked… that’s an interesting question. Coming into this, I had a decent advantage. I could read code, and I knew the basics. (taking classes out of order seems to be how I roll). In webdev, I was always lost, always confused, but It taught me what I was looking at. The moment code clicked was about halfway through…I think it was doing css. Once I could see things look the way I wanted, rather than old 90s era sites…I kinda started seeing code. I could see how the code would look, when I saw sites.

The Pit:

The hardest assignment for me was the field guide. I was doing lots of ‘not inside the box’ type things. I wanted it to look how I designed it, and I didn’t care what it took to learn it. (my m.o. is obviously not normal). I got the page starting to look like I wanted, and I think I asked copilot to check it all, or add to the main page, and everything disappeared. I learned a lesson that day…save. Often. So I had to totally rebuild everything. But I it went quicker the second time.

The Pivot:

Compared to my original “moods and vibes” I think my final portfolio stayed true. I didn’t really change the vibe, and I kept the bright galaxy type, and had the muted background. Honestly I think my vibe stayed through most of my projects. The one thing I did change was the scrapbook/paper type feel. I did that for the field guide, and really liked it, so I kept that.

The Process:

What would I change…hmm… I would always change my time management. I’m horrible at it. I get really tired after doing life…and kids always come first. So, sometimes I fall behind, unfortunately. Reading things that aren’t ‘for pleasure’ (like a book I am invested in) usually makes my brain go wonky. It’s hard for me to pay attention or retain information. I try to listen to school books whenever I can because it helps me focus. I usually have no problems asking for help, but my problem is I don’t know when I need it…until the whole thing crashes and I have to start over…oops.

The Future:

This hybrid skill set will help my future because it opens up another avenue. After webdev, I was absolutely certain that I wanted nothing to do with coding. But after code+design, I feel a little more comfortable with coding and design. I definitely see why code+design needs to come before dev. I also liked using ai to help. I’m glad I had those classes back to back. (I probably would’ve lost my mind having them together though)
